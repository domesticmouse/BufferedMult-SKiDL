Step 1. Doepfer A-100 3U × 4HP Geometry & the +6.48 mm Thonkiconn Trap

In Lesson 0005, we placed our 12 vertical WQP-PJ398SM (Thonkiconn) jacks (J2–J13) on a 20.0 × 108.0 mm main PCB spanning X = 20.0..40.0 mm and Y = 20.0..128.0 mm. Now we need a 3U × 4HP Eurorack Front Panel (20.0 × 128.5 mm per the Doepfer A-100 standard) that bolts directly over those 12 jack bushings.

Now, you might assume we can just drill front-panel holes at the exact (X, Y) coordinates where we placed the jack footprints in Lesson 0005. Turns out, if you do that, your front panel will be off by nearly six and a half millimetres and won't fit over a single jack! The reason this happens comes down to two mechanical details:

  • Footprint Origin (0, 0) vs. Bushing Shaft Centre (0, +6.48 mm): Inside KiCad 10's Connector_Audio:Jack_3.5mm_QingPu_WQP-PJ398SM_Vertical_CircularHoles footprint, the origin (0, 0) sits at Pad S (Sleeve) at the top of the jack body. The threaded M6 bushing shaft where your patch cable plugs in (fp_circle) sits +6.48 mm lower in Y (at 0.0, +6.48 mm when rotation is 0°).
  • Vertical PCB-to-Panel Centring (10.25 mm Rail Margin): Centring our 108.0 mm tall main PCB behind a 128.5 mm tall 3U panel leaves (128.5 - 108.0) / 2 = 10.25 mm of margin at both the top and bottom edges—clearing standard ~7.5 mm Eurorack mounting rail lips with 2.75 mm to spare. Because the main PCB starts at (20.0, 20.0 mm), the top-left corner of the centred front panel sits at (20.0, 9.75 mm) in main-PCB coordinates.

Step 2. Rotation-Aware Offset Math & Copper Shield Planes

In KiCad, footprint rotations are measured counter-clockwise (CCW) in degrees while the board's Y-axis points downward. Our helper function rotate_offset(cx, cy, rot_deg) rotates the local bushing vector (0.0, 6.48) by any footprint orientation angle, and compute_panel_coordinates() subtracts the panel's top-left origin (20.0, 9.75 mm):

So, when we order the front panel as a standard 1.6 mm FR-4 PCB (BufferedMult_Panel.kicad_pcb) from the same fab house, pouring solid copper planes across F.Cu and B.Cu with exposed rear solder-mask rings (B.Mask) around the 6.2 mm jack holes gives us two hardware wins for free:

  1. Automatic Chassis Shielding: Tightening the M6 knurled jack nuts presses the grounded jack bushings against the exposed copper rings, bonding both copper planes to GND as a full-height Faraday shield over your op-amps.
  2. Zero Light Bleed & Extra Rigidity: Solid dual-layer copper blocks case LEDs from shining through the fibreglass substrate and stiffens the 1.6 mm panel against patching flex.
