Step 1. Anatomy of a Real-World Hardware Design Review

I could pretend that once SKiDL's ERC() printed 0 errors, 0 warnings, our physical PCB layout worked on the very first try. Spoiler: it didn't.

And honestly, that's the best part of bringing software-style verification to hardware design. Passing ERC() proves that your Python netlist is electrically sound, but physical PCB layout introduces geometric and analog traps that only a post-layout Design Rules Check (DRC) and pre-fabrication audit can catch.

During the engineering review of BufferedMult.kicad_pcb, running kicad-cli pcb drc --refill-zones alongside our automated PCB, Gerber, and BOM analysers caught and resolved 9 real issues before we sent a single file to the fab house:

Real-World Pre-Fab Findings Caught & Fixed Before Ordering
Category Initial Finding Root Cause & Engineering Fix
Critical Open Net U1 Pin 5 (INB+) unrouted Stage B's non-inverting input pin was left unrouted on B.Cu, which would have left OUT_1A floating. Routed U1:5 to CH1_BUF (0 unconnected items).
Isolated GND Island C5 Pad 2 disconnected from main GND On a narrow 20.0 mm (4HP) strip, dense through-hole jack pad clearances pinched off the copper pour around C5 Pad 2 into an isolated island. Fixed by adding GND stitching vias (12 total GND vias tying F.Cu and B.Cu pours together).
Power Spine Width +12V / -12V routed at 0.20 mm Signal-width power traces add unnecessary series impedance. Assigned SKiDL's NetClass("Power", trace_width=0.5, clearance=0.25) and widened both power spines to 0.50 mm, while pulling the -12V spine inward to X = 20.8 mm to clear the board edge.
Op-Amp Phase Inversion Legacy JFET TL074CDR (C12594) Classic JFET-input TL074C op-amps suffer from input phase inversion if an external signal drives an input near the negative rail—latching the output to +V. Upgraded to TI's modern CMOS TL074HIDR (TL074H, LCSC C2878713), which eliminates phase inversion, lowers offset to 1 mV typ, and increases slew rate to 20 V/µs.
SMT vs. THT BOM CSV THT jacks in general BOM export Uploading a raw KiCad BOM that includes THT parts (J1–J13, C1, C2) causes JLCPCB's SMT parser to flag missing LCSC codes or quote manual soldering fees. Using our filtered buffered_multiple_jlcpcb_bom.csv exports strictly the 29 B.Cu SMD parts across 7 unique lines.

Step 2. Distinguishing Real Blockers from Benign 4HP Form-Factor Warnings

Turns out, automated PCB and EMC analysers also flag geometric heuristics that are completely intentional for a compact 4HP (20.0 mm) module. Knowing why an alert is benign is just as important as fixing real bugs:

  • Jack Courtyard at 0.0 mm from Board Edge (PM-002): Each WQP-PJ398SM (Thonkiconn) housing is 10.0 mm wide. Placing two columns centred at X = 25.0 mm and X = 35.0 mm on a board spanning X = 20.0..40.0 mm aligns the plastic jack bodies flush with the left and right PCB edges by design, while every copper pad sits safely inside the outline.
  • Series Schottky Drop vs. Unclipped Output Headroom: Our 1N5819WS reverse-polarity protection diodes (D1, D2) drop ~0.38 V per rail, supplying ±11.62 V (23.24 V span) to U1–U3. With TL074H's output stage, unclipped output swing is ~±10.4 V—comfortably covering Eurorack's ±5 V audio/LFO and 0..+10 V CV standards while guaranteeing reverse-plug protection.
