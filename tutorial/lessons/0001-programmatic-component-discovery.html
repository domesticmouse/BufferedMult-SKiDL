How Did buffered_multiple.py Know Every Symbol, Pin, and LCSC Number?

In traditional GUI schematic capture, you hunt through nested menus to find a symbol, drop it on a sheet, and hope its pin numbers match your footprint. When designing circuits in Python with SKiDL, you never guess symbol names or pin numbers—you query KiCad 10's libraries and the JLCPCB catalog directly from Python before writing your subcircuits.

Look at lines 60–80 of buffered_multiple.py: every footprint constant (FP_OPAMP_SOIC14, FP_JACK_EURORACK) and every LCSC part number (LCSC_U_TL074H_SOIC14 = "C2878713", LCSC_R_100K_0603 = "C25803") comes from a four-step programmatic lookup pipeline.

1. Discovering KiCad 10 Symbols & Pinouts from Python

SKiDL provides search("keyword") to scan KiCad's .kicad_sym libraries and show("Library", "SymbolName") (or inspecting Part(lib, sym, dest=TEMPLATE).pins) to list every pin's exact .num, .name, and electrical function (PASSIVE, INPUT, OUTPUT, PWRIN).

When we run tutorial/apps/0001-programmatic-component-discovery/discover_parts.py against KiCad 10, three critical hardware insights immediately jump out:

  1. Why TL074 amplifier pins use u.p[n] while power pins use u["V+"]: Inspecting Amplifier_Operational:TL074 reveals 14 pins across 4 op-amp units plus power: pin 1 ('', OUTPUT), pin 2 ('-', INPUT), pin 3 ('+', INPUT), pin 4 ('V+', PWRIN), pin 5 ('+', INPUT), pin 6 ('-', INPUT), pin 7 ('', OUTPUT)... Because all four op-amp units share the exact same pin names ('+', '-', and empty ''), indexing u["+"] would match all four non-inverting inputs at once! Using the Numeric Pin Accessor (part.p[n])—such as u.p[3], u.p[1], u.p[2]—targets physical SOIC-14 package pin numbers unambiguously, while u["V+"] (pin 4) and u["V-"] (pin 11) are unique and readable by name.
  2. Why AudioJack2_SwitchT pins are indexed as j_in["S"], j_in["T"], and j_in["TN"]: Inspecting Connector_Audio:AudioJack2_SwitchT shows pin S ('', PASSIVE), pin T ('', PASSIVE), pin TN ('', PASSIVE). On this symbol, the letters S (Sleeve), T (Tip), and TN (Switched Tip Normal Contact (TN)) are the actual pin numbers (`pin.num`) rather than integers `1, 2, 3`!
  3. Why C_Polarized bulk caps use c_bulk[1] and c_bulk[2]: Inspecting Device:C_Polarized shows pin 1 ('', PASSIVE), pin 2 ('', PASSIVE)—where pin `1` is the positive anode and pin `2` is the negative cathode.

Critical Gotcha: Always Use part.fields["LCSC"], Never part.LCSC

In SKiDL, SkidlBaseObject.__setattr__ only writes an attribute into part.fields if that key already exists in part.fields. If you write r_pd.LCSC = "C25803" or pass LCSC="C25803" to Part(...), Python creates a plain instance attribute that is silently ignored by generate_xml() and generate_netlist()! Always assign custom catalog metadata into the Part Fields Dictionary (part.fields): r_pd.fields["LCSC"] = "C25803". Even better, any fields set on a Template Part (dest=TEMPLATE) are automatically copied to every part cloned from that template.
